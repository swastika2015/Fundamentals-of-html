The universe is the vast, 13.8-billion-year-old totality of all space, time, matter, and energy, originating from a hot, dense state known as the Big Bang. It is expanding, likely forever, with an observable diameter of at least 93 billion light-years, composed of dark energy (68%), dark matter (27%), and ordinary matter (5%).

Space telescopes are orbital observatories designed to observe astronomical objects without atmospheric interference—such as absorption, distortion, and light pollution. Launched above Earth’s atmosphere, these instruments (like Hubble and JWST) provide high-resolution data across various wavelengths, including ultraviolet, visible, and infrared, enabling deeper, clearer views of the cosmos
