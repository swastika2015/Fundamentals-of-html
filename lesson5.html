Facts about lions

Lions (Panthera leo) are highly social, vulnerable big cats, with roughly 23,000 left in the wild. Known as "kings of the jungle" but living mostly in savannahs, they are the only cats that live in large, cooperative groups called prides. Lionesses do 85–90% of the hunting, while males protect the territory.

Facts about tigers

Tigers are the largest wild cats in the world, renowned for their solitary nature, unique striped coats, and status as endangered apex predators. Native to Asia, they are exceptional swimmers, possess powerful hunting abilities, and rely on stealth, with fewer than 5,500 estimated to be left in the wild.

Facts about giraffes

Giraffes are the world's tallest land mammals, reaching up to 5.5 meters (18 feet) in height, inhabiting African savannahs. They are herbivores, primarily eating acacia leaves, and have unique coat patterns similar to human fingerprints. Giraffes stand while eating, sleeping, and giving birth, with calves often standing within an hour of birth.

Facts about horses

Horses are highly social herbivores that can sleep both standing up and lying down, with a 25–30 year lifespan. They have nearly 360-degree vision and the largest eyes of any land mammal. Foals can stand and run within hours of birth, and horses communicate through vocalizations and body language.
